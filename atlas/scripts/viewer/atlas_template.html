Morpho Angular Atlas

Thirty-one Morpho specimens measured at incidence angles I from −80° to 80° (10° steps) and collection angles C from −90° to 90° (5° steps). Each map cell is one geometry (I, C), coloured as the eye would see it in daylight (CIE D65 illuminant, 2° standard observer), computed from the cleaned spectrum.

Colour maps by species

All maps share one exposure, so brightness is comparable across species. The dashed line is the ideal specular geometry C = −I. Hatched cells are the blind zone where the detector blocks the source (C ≈ I); red cells are geometries that were never measured. Select a species to explore it.

Explorer

Hover the map to read a geometry; click a cell to see its raw and cleaned spectrum. The five remeasured species also offer their previous sweep for comparison. Every symbol is defined in Definitions.

Gruson model fits

Angular fits of brightness and hue with the model of Gruson et al. (2019), from the dedicated sweep sessions (31 Aug – 21 Oct 2026, accepted fits only). The colour map shows where each sweep sits on the angular grid: circles are brightness sweeps (constant I + C, across the specular band) and diamonds are hue sweeps (constant I − C, along it). Hover a sweep anywhere to highlight it everywhere; click a chip to hide it.

Gruson parameters across species

Mean ± SD over accepted sweeps for each parameter, sorted by value. The selected species is highlighted; click any row to open that species.

Definitions

Every quantity shown in the Atlas. Spectral descriptors are computed on the cleaned spectrum R(λ), in % of the white reference, over 400–700 nm with negative values set to zero. Brightness, hue and chroma variables follow Montgomerie (2006), with the same definitions as summary.rspec in the R package pavo.

Cleaning pipeline

Steps applied by pipeline2.py, in order. Parameters are stored in parametros.json.

  1. Header parsing and repair. I and C are read regardless of token order. Swapped I/C labels (aega), typos such as -60+C, -8OC, 70I40C, -2+5C, a missing C letter, 990C, and off-grid angles (4C, -79C) are fixed and logged.
  2. Copies and duplicates. Columns that are byte-identical copies of another spectrum are dropped; this is how the original "negative" files of cisseis and amphitryon turned out to be copies of the positive ones. Both were replaced by corrected files, and the replacement is logged. Repeated geometries with different content keep the column from the file whose incidence sign matches.
  3. Hot pixels. Pixels that spike in more than 30% of a species' spectra are interpolated, together with their immediate neighbours.
  4. Random spikes. Hampel filter (7-pixel window, 6 MAD).
  5. Smoothing. Savitzky–Golay, order 3, 25-pixel window (~14 nm). Morpho peaks are 60–100 nm wide, so their shape is preserved.
  6. Range and resampling. 380–780 nm on a 1 nm grid. Outside that range the signal is mostly reference noise.
  7. Colour descriptors. Standard Montgomerie (2006) variables with the same definitions as pavo::summary.rspec, over 400–700 nm with negative values set to zero: brightness B1, B2, B3; hue H1; chroma S6, S8. Map colours are computed with the CIE D65 illuminant and 2° standard observer and shown in sRGB.
  8. Measurement checks. Row drift, negative baseline, reflectance above the white standard and likely mislabelled spectra are flagged, not corrected. These are data-integrity checks, not colour descriptors.
